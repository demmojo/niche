# Strategy Toolbox: User Value Proposition Brainstorm

## What We're Exploring

The current documentation and plan for the "Strategy Toolbox" (formerly Analytical Template Library) are highly technical (focusing on React Query, background syncs, discriminated unions). We need to articulate the _user-centric value proposition_. Why does a Product Manager or Founder use this? What is the expected result, and what is the best-case scenario?

## Why This Matters (The User's Problem)

Founders and Product Managers at early-stage startups often build products based on gut feeling, leading to wasted engineering cycles on features nobody wants. They know they _should_ do market research, JTBD analysis, and assumption testing, but it's incredibly time-consuming to gather the data, analyze it, and translate it into actionable product decisions.

## The Core Value Proposition

**De-risk your product before writing a single line of code.**
The Strategy Toolbox acts as an AI-powered Chief Product Officer. It doesn't just give you a blank canvas; it brings the market data to you, applies battle-tested product frameworks (like Blue Ocean and JTBD), and tells you exactly where the gaps are.

## What is the Expectation of the Result?

When a user clicks into a tool (e.g., "Competitor Feature Gap & Sentiment Matrix"):

1. **Instant Clarity:** They don't have to enter data manually. The system has already ingested competitor reviews and sentiment.
2. **Actionable Visuals:** They see a clear, data-backed visualization (e.g., a quadrant showing high-importance, low-satisfaction features).
3. **Strategic Direction:** They get a definitive recommendation: "Build feature X because Competitor Y's users hate their implementation of it."
4. **Immediate Next Steps:** They can seamlessly turn that insight into an experiment (Fake Door test) or generate a PRD.

## The Best Case Scenario (The "Magic" Moment)

A founder has an idea for a new B2B SaaS tool.

1. They enter their competitors.
2. They open the **JTBD Opportunity Landscape**. The AI highlights a specific "Job" that is highly important but terribly served by incumbents.
3. They use **Assumption Mapping** to identify the riskiest assumption about this new feature.
4. They launch a **Pretotyping (Fake Door) Validation** campaign directly from the platform.
5. Within 24 hours, they have empirical data proving demand. They click "Generate MVP," and the system outputs the exact code and specs needed.
   **Result:** They found product-market fit on a feature before spending thousands of dollars on engineering.

## Key Decisions

- **Pivot Documentation Tone:** Update the `proposal.md` and `plan.md` to lead with this value proposition. Technical architecture (BFF, Zod, React Query) should be framed as _enablers_ of this user experience, not the main focal point.
- **Focus on the "So What?":** Every tool description in the UI and docs must answer: "What business decision will this help me make?"

## Open Questions

- None. This is a repositioning of the existing feature set to be purely user-centric.
